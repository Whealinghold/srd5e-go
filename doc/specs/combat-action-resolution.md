> Go notation used below. A name in lowerCamelCase (a field, command argument or enum member) stands for the exported PascalCase Go identifier (`attackerID` is the field `AttackerID`). A trailing `?` marks an optional value (the zero value or a `(T, bool)` pair). `[]T` is a slice; `set of T` is a collection without duplicates. A `(T, error)` pair replaces an accepted/rejected result pair: the nil-error case is the accepted case and the error is the typed rejection, satisfying `errors.Is(err, srderr.ErrInvalidInput)`; a parser reports every problem at once through `errors.Join`. Rule outcomes (refusals, not-possible stops) stay result values. A use case is a struct with `Run(ctx, cmd)`; its `error` is non-nil only for a port failure.

## 1. Purpose and scope

The sibling `combat` specs are pure and know nothing of each other (shared FR-015, FR-026). This spec is the **one place that wires them**: use cases take an `Encounter` (and an `ActionLedger`), run one thing a combatant does, and return the new state and the ordered events. Each composes the root's methods, pure domain services and sibling value-object operations, and rolls dice through its own `dice.Roller`. **No use case calls a use case** (shared FR-044; architecture §4), so each call records only its own signals (FR-007). This spec adds wiring, no mechanics (shared FR-048).

It provides, for every "wiring is open" deferral of the siblings (section 9): the eight use cases (`PerformAttack`, `PerformUnarmedStrike`, `TakeCombatantAction`, `MoveCombatant`, `AdvanceCombatTurn`, `RollCombatantDeathSavingThrow`, `AttemptToStabilizeCombatant`, `DealDamageToCombatant`; section 6), the use cases of the domain-only specs (4.20), the stat-block, Parry and gear wiring (4.20); the chaining of a tracker step, an attack result, condition effects, Grapple/Shove/posture/shared-space consequences, damage into Concentration, and the ledger (4.2 to 4.13, 4.18); one ordered event stream per call (4.15); and the scenario for `examples/combat` (or `Example` functions) (section 8). State in, new state and events out: a call is **atomic**; when it stops, the encounter in the result is the one that came in and no event is produced.

**Out of scope:**

- **Rules**, which stay in their owning spec (package decisions are listed in section 9); class features, Mastery effects, mounts, jumping, flight, Hide, Search, Study, Influence (composition in section 9); the Grapple record and escape (grappling.md; shared FR-029, FR-048); the mechanics of the Attack action and Help (attack-and-help-actions.md), which this spec only threads (4.18).
- **Positions, range, distance.** The engine has no map; the host supplies distances and sight in an `AttackScene`. The engine never detects that a move provokes an Opportunity Attack, who is in reach, or that a Grappled creature was dragged out of reach (SRD 5.2.1, "Opportunity Attacks", p. 15).
- **Starting a combat** (`RollInitiative`, `EstablishInitiativeOrder`, `StartEncounter`): host decisions between the calls (initiative-and-actions.md FR-019). **Spell effects and casting** (spellcasting.md, spell-effects.md, magical-effects.md; shared FR-048): the casting state is not held by the encounter (Q-22, Q-49). **Rests, Hit Point Dice, reviving the dead** (encounter-aggregate.md section 1). **Persistence and display** (the host).
- **The end of a knock-out.** "Knocking Out a Creature" (SRD 5.2.1, p. 17, repeated p. 184): the creature starts a Short Rest at whose end Unconscious ends, or earlier on regaining Hit Points or on first aid (an action, DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine)). The knock-out is applied through `MeleeAttackKnockOut` (FR-037); no condition source marker exists for 0.1.0 (encounter-aggregate.md FR-092): after healing, first aid or the Short Rest the host removes Unconscious with `removeCondition`, and the marker is deferred to a conditions.md feature. `AttemptToStabilizeCombatant` stops with `NotDying` for a knocked-out creature at 1 Hit Point (FR-072). Q-41.

## 4. Requirements

Stable ids; never renumber; new ids go at the end. "The root" is the `Encounter` aggregate's methods (encounter-aggregate.md section 6). A "tracker operation" is a `TurnTracker` method (`takeAction`, `takeBonusAction`, `takeReaction`, `interactWithObject`, `move`, `changePosture`; initiative-and-actions.md FR-140 to FR-148); a "step" is its `TrackerStep`: `TrackerStepApplied` (the new `TurnTracker` and events) or an error (TurnRejection). `ResolveAttackResult` and `ResolveUnarmedStrikeResult` are attacks.md's results, used in the use-case files only. A "stop" is the rejection or problem held by a `...Rejected` or `...Invalid` result.

## 5. Model

**Domain services** (`combat/`, stateless, pure, silent; none rolls a die, holds state, imports the use-case files or takes an use-case-layer type, FR-121):

- `ResolutionPrecedence`: the order of FR-010 over the encounter's facts and the other checks' results.
- `AttackConditionInputs`: from the two `ConditionSet`s, the `AttackScene`, the tracker's `DodgeBenefits` and the target's `ConditionActionAvailability`: the circumstances, modifiers, `AutomaticCritical` and targeting permission (FR-027 to FR-035), the condition-derived Speed 0 that cancels Dodge (FR-125) and the `SaveAdjustments` (FR-045). With `MovementChecks` and `ConsequenceRules`, the only user of `ConditionEffects` (FR-006).
- `DamageHandoff`: the `Damage` list, `DamageDelivery` and `HitKind` (FR-036, FR-037); never sees an attack result type. `UnarmedEffects`: the condition to apply (Grappled, Prone) or the recorded push (FR-046, FR-047).
- `MovementChecks`: the Speed 0 (move and `DropProne`), Prone-only-crawl and posture checks (FR-057 to FR-061, FR-124).
- `ConsequenceRules`: who needs `MovementSpeedsReviewBecameDue` (FR-085) or `GrappleCheckBecameDue` (FR-120); the Concentration decision of FR-078 steps 1, 2, 4 and the amount of FR-132 (step 3 needs a die and is the use case's). Calls `ConcentrationSaveDc.ForDamage` (FR-131).

**Composed, not owned** (FR-007): the `Encounter` root (`applyTurnChange`, `advanceTurn`, `applyDamage`, `applyCondition`, `removeCondition`, `applyDeathSavingThrow`, `stabilize`); the tracker operations (initiative-and-actions.md); `AttackFlow` and the attack domain services (attacks.md FR-127); `d20.Resolver` (d20-tests.md FR-069); `ConcentrationRules`, `ConcentrationSaveDc` (spellcasting.md); `AttackActionProgress`, `HelpBenefits` (attack-and-help-actions.md). None is a use case of another spec.

**Invariants.** A rejected or invalid call changes nothing (FR-009). Only the root commits, only sibling values change the ledger, no use case calls a use case, condition rules are asked only here (FR-006, FR-007, FR-008; architecture-guardrails.md FR-097). Domain services, events, `ConcentrationInput`, `ConcentrationCheckOutcome` and event payloads hold no use-case-layer type (FR-121). `AttackScene` distance is 0 or more and equals the attack's; an Opportunity Attack is a melee weapon attack or Unarmed Strike against its provoker, by another combatant who sees it; knock-out only with a melee attack; no Attack action with a spell; means match the kind: all in constructors (FR-097, FR-135 to FR-142).

**Failures.** Results: every `ResolutionRejection`, a miss, a failed save, a blocked effect; `...Invalid` for an unknown id. `srderr.ErrInvalidInput`: malformed commands (FR-097, FR-135 to FR-144). a panic (unreachable branch): an `EncounterRejection` the stop order makes unreachable (FR-014, FR-100). `*srderr.PortError`: `*srderr.PortError` only, propagated (FR-095). Nothing in the domain calls a port.

## 6. Interface

All use cases are structs with one method, `Run(ctx context.Context, cmd <UseCase>Command) (<UseCase>Result, error)`; the returned `error` is non-nil only for a port failure (a `*srderr.PortError`, FR-095), never for a rule outcome. Each constructor `NewX` takes, where a die is rolled, a `Roller` first (a one-method interface declared in `combat`, satisfied by any `dice.Roller`) and `opts ...Option`; the options are `WithTelemetry(telemetry.Telemetry)` (default `telemetry.Noop`, FR-002) and `WithClock(func() time.Time)` (default `time.Now`, used only to time the duration histogram); nothing else is injected (FR-007). A constructor returns an error satisfying `errors.Is(err, srderr.ErrInvalidInput)` for a nil roller. The use cases are values safe for concurrent use when the roller is.

## 7. Observability

Answers to the seven questions (`doc/rules/observability.md` §2).
1. **What can go wrong?** The dice source fails (`*srderr.PortError`). The host sends ids the encounter does not hold, or commands that do not fit the state (spent action, Incapacitated actor, dead target, impossible attack, attack past the allowance, Speed 0 forced by a condition, Prone without crawl). The host sends a `SpellcastingState` or `ActionLedger` that does not match the combat. The host forgets the prompts (`DyingCombatantTurnStarted`, `MovementSpeedsReviewBecameDue`) and drifts from the rules. A rule interaction resolves unexpectedly (Advantage and Disadvantage cancelling).
2. **How would an operator know?** A dice failure is span error status plus `srd5e.combat.errors` with `error.type`; an unknown id is a result of class `invalid` (span error status, not counted as an error); other stops are class `refused` with the closed `srd5e.combat.result`. A forgotten prompt shows as a widening gap between `srd5e.combat.events` counts of `dying_combatant_turn_started` / `movement_speeds_review_became_due` and the `srd5e.combat.operations` counts of `roll_combatant_death_saving_throw` / `update_movement_speeds` (initiative-and-actions.md). A mismatched `ActionLedger` is a `ledger_refused` result. A mismatched `SpellcastingState` (another character's) is **not detectable**: the host holds it beside the character (Q-12), the encounter holds none; the only symptom is a `concentration_checks` mix that looks wrong.
3. **What is needed to diagnose?** The result carries the whole breakdown (attack, damage resolutions, save, Concentration outcome, the stop with its data) and the ordered events; telemetry carries operation, result class and name, event kinds, death cause and Concentration outcome. Combatant ids are never attributes.
4. **What is counted or timed?** The shared signals (combat-foundations.md) count calls by operation and result, events by kind, errors by type and time each use case. This spec adds Concentration checks by outcome, increments the shared `srd5e.combat.deaths` by cause and, for the use cases of 4.20, takes the owners' result values (their section 7 tables are the source, FR-170).
5. **Is existing instrumentation still correct?** Yes. No use case calls another (FR-007; shared FR-044), so no span is nested, `srd5e.d20_test.*` and the siblings' use-case signals do not fire for calls made here (only for a host's direct `ResolveD20Test`), no error is counted twice, and the encounter's use-case signals do not fire (the root is called as domain code); `srd5e.combat.deaths` is emitted by whichever use case produced the death (FR-102), never doubled.
6. **What should a consumer monitor?** Any `srd5e.combat.errors` with `error.type=dice_roller_error`; any `invalid` result (host out of step); the share of `refused` per operation and result (a high `attack_not_possible` or `ledger_refused` share means wrong distances, cover, sizes or attack counts); `broken` against `held` in `concentration_checks` (dice bias or changed save modifier); `ended_incapacitated` and `ended_dead` against all checks (characters dropping out of Concentration for a reason other than a failed save, or a wrong `SpellcastingState`); the gaps of question 2, which should stay near zero over a session; a duration regression.
7. **Is anything now noisy?** No log: the span and counters carry what a `LevelDebug` summary would, and a stop is a normal rule outcome (observability §7). Counters are per call and per event, never per modifier or per combatant loop.

Domain services and events are **silent by design**: pure, returning what happened as values (observability §3). Only the use cases produce signals, through `Instrumenter`. The shared signals (`srd5e.combat.<operation>` spans, `operations`, `operation_duration`, `events`, `errors`) and `srd5e.combat.deaths` (FR-102) are combat-foundations.md's and hit-points-and-damage.md's; this spec adds its operations and result names to their closed sets. Closed value sets contributed: `srd5e.combat.operation` (the eight names of FR-099 and the 25 of FR-170); `srd5e.combat.result` (`completed`, the stop names of FR-100 and the values of each owner's section 7 table and FR-169, FR-170); `srd5e.combat.concentration_outcome` (`held`, `broken`, `ended_incapacitated`, `ended_dead`).

| Signal | Type | Attributes | Purpose | Suggested monitoring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `srd5e.combat.concentration_checks` | counter, `{check}` | `srd5e.combat.concentration_outcome` | How often damage ends Concentration, and how (failed save, Incapacitated, death). | Watch `broken` against `held`; a sudden shift suggests a dice bias or a changed save modifier input. |
